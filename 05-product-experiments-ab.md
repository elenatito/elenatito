# Product case: A/B testing and activation funnel

**Context:** B2C fintech / children's banking product  
**Role:** Product Lead / Product Owner  
**Type:** product analytics, experimentation, customer journey optimization

## Problem

The product had several points of friction between card issuance, activation and the next key customer actions. The challenge was to understand whether the main losses were caused by the product flow itself or by the communication strategy after issuance.

## My task

Identify the highest-impact opportunities in the customer journey, formulate testable hypotheses and use experiments to decide which changes should become part of the product roadmap.

## What I did

### 1. Mapped the funnel

I analysed the path from issuance to activation and identified the points where customers dropped out or failed to complete the target action.

### 2. Converted observations into hypotheses

Instead of treating every friction point as a product problem, I formulated separate hypotheses for the interface, customer communication and behavioural triggers.

### 3. Ran A/B tests

Selected examples:

- activation of the children's profile: **32% → 40%**;
- limit-setting conversion: **18% → 33%**;
- omnichannel activation: **40% → 43%**.

### 4. Added a holdout group

For the omnichannel activation experiment, I used a **5–10% holdout group without communication** to estimate the incremental effect rather than attributing all observed activity to the campaign.

### 5. Used the results to make a product decision

The experiment showed that push + SMS was the main scalable driver of activation, while calling was not economically justified for the mass segment. This changed the communication strategy and helped focus the product on channels with better expected impact.

## Results

The experiments produced measurable improvements in activation and conversion and, more importantly, gave the product team a framework for separating true incremental impact from activity that would have happened without intervention.

## What this case demonstrates

- hypothesis-driven product management;
- funnel thinking;
- experiment design;
- understanding of incremental impact and holdout groups;
- ability to translate data into a product decision;
- balancing customer outcome with unit economics.

## My role

I formulated the hypotheses, worked with analytics and product teams on experiment design, interpreted the results and translated the findings into product and communication decisions. Implementation was carried out by the relevant development, analytics and communications teams.
